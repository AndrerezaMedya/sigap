# SIGAP UI/UX Design System & Experience Direction
## Smart Intelligent Guide for Activity Planning
### AI-Powered City Decision Engine

---

# Design Philosophy

SIGAP is not a traditional weather application.

It is not designed to function as:
- a technical environmental dashboard,
- a GIS-heavy monitoring platform,
- or a generic AI chatbot.

SIGAP is designed as:

# An Ambient Urban Decision Intelligence Experience

The platform should feel:
- intelligent,
- calm,
- contextual,
- trustworthy,
- modern,
- and human-centered.

The primary objective of the interface is not to expose raw environmental data.

The primary objective is:

# Helping users make confident real-world decisions.

---

# Core UX Principle

## “Tell me what I should know.”

Instead of:
- overwhelming users with technical information,
- excessive charts,
- or complex GIS layers,

SIGAP should:
- synthesize complexity,
- simplify interpretation,
- and present actionable insights.

The platform must prioritize:
- recommendations,
- contextual reasoning,
- and decision support.

---

# Product Personality

SIGAP should feel like:
- a calm urban intelligence assistant,
- an environmental co-pilot,
- and a trusted decision-support layer.

The interface should avoid:
- cyberpunk aesthetics,
- hacker-style dashboards,
- excessive animations,
- or highly technical visual clutter.

Instead, the experience should feel:
- elegant,
- breathable,
- spatially clean,
- and emotionally reassuring.

---

# User Experience Goals

Users should feel:

## 1. Understood
The system already understands:
- their location,
- environmental context,
- mobility conditions,
- and activity intent.

## 2. Guided
The platform helps users:
- choose better timing,
- avoid environmental risks,
- and make smarter decisions.

## 3. Informed Without Overload
Users receive:
- concise insights,
- contextual reasoning,
- and practical recommendations.

## 4. Calm & Confident
The interface should reduce uncertainty,
not increase anxiety.

---

# Overall Experience Direction

The overall product experience should resemble:

- modern AI-native applications,
- premium mobility platforms,
- intelligent urban operating systems,
- and next-generation contextual assistants.

Inspirational qualities include:
- Apple Weather simplicity,
- Google Material design clarity,
- Tesla-style information hierarchy,
- Linear’s modern cleanliness,
- and Google Maps contextual intelligence.

---

# Primary Interface Philosophy

# Recommendation-First UI

SIGAP should prioritize:
- decision outputs,
- activity recommendations,
- and environmental interpretation.

NOT:
- raw datasets,
- excessive maps,
- or complex analytical controls.

The interface hierarchy should always be:

1. Decision
2. Reasoning
3. Supporting data

---

# Homepage Experience

The homepage should immediately answer:

# “What should I know right now?”

The landing experience should NOT begin with:
- a full-screen map,
- technical dashboards,
- dense charts,
- or environmental graphs.

Instead, users should first see:

# A Smart Decision Panel

This panel acts as:
- the emotional center,
- the AI intelligence surface,
- and the primary recommendation layer.

---

# Smart Decision Panel

The decision panel should include:

## Location Context
Example:
- Bandung • Dago
- Jakarta • Sudirman

## Environmental Status Summary
Example:
- “Currently comfortable for outdoor activity.”
- “Heavy congestion and rainfall expected soon.”
- “Air quality is declining this afternoon.”

## AI Recommendation
Example:
- “Recommended to depart before 16:20.”
- “Outdoor exercise is less recommended this evening.”
- “Avoid flood-prone routes in southern areas.”

## Reasoning Summary
Example:
- rainfall trend,
- AQI deterioration,
- traffic increase,
- environmental warnings.

## Confidence or Risk Indicator
Simple visual indication:
- Safe
- Moderate
- Caution
- High Risk

---

SIGAP should be activity-oriented.

Users interact through real-world intentions.

Examples:
- commuting,
- motorcycle travel,
- jogging,
- outdoor events,
- walking,
- family activities,
- tourism,
- public transportation.

The UI should provide:

# Quick Activity Selection

Examples:
- 🏍 Ride Motorcycle
- 🚶 Walk
- 🚗 Drive
- 🏃 Jogging
- 🎪 Outdoor Event
- 🚲 Cycling
- 👨‍👩‍👧 Family Activity

The AI recommendation engine should adapt based on the selected activity.

---

# Smart Timeline Experience

One of SIGAP’s key “wow moments” is:

# Time-Based Decision Optimization

The system should visually explain:
- optimal departure windows,
- worsening conditions,
- congestion periods,
- rainfall timing,
- and environmental transitions.

Example:

| Time | Situation |
|------|------------|
| 07:00 | Traffic congestion high |
| 07:15 | Most optimal departure window |
| 08:00 | Rain intensity increases |

The visualization should feel:
- elegant,
- lightweight,
- and easy to understand.

Avoid overly technical charts.

---

# Maps & Geospatial Experience

Maps are important,
but should function as:

# Secondary Intelligence Surfaces

Maps should NOT dominate the entire experience.

Maps are used when users need:
- route analysis,
- flood visualization,
- AQI zones,
- traffic overlays,
- environmental hotspots,
- or contextual spatial understanding.

The map experience should feel:
- focused,
- contextual,
- and purposeful.

Avoid:
- GIS-style visual overload,
- excessive layer controls,
- and enterprise dashboard complexity.

---

# AI Interaction Philosophy

SIGAP is NOT primarily a chatbot product.

The AI experience should feel:
- proactive,
- contextual,
- and ambient.

Users should not always need to ask questions.

The platform should already provide:
- recommendations,
- warnings,
- and insights automatically.

Chat interaction may exist,
but should function as:
- a secondary enhancement layer,
not the core interface.

---

# Information Hierarchy

The platform should organize information in this order:

## Level 1 — Decision
Examples:
- Safe
- Comfortable
- Less Recommended
- High Risk

## Level 2 — Contextual Reasoning
Examples:
- due to heavy rainfall,
- increasing AQI,
- traffic congestion,
- or flood warnings.

## Level 3 — Detailed Information
Examples:
- weather graphs,
- AQI values,
- maps,
- radar layers,
- and analytics.

The user should never be forced to interpret technical details first.

---

# Visual Design Direction

## Color Philosophy

SIGAP should feel:
- clean,
- modern,
- trustworthy,
- and environmentally aware.

Recommended base palette:
- white,
- off-white,
- slate,
- soft gray,
- muted blue,
- soft green.

Accent colors should represent:
- risk,
- urgency,
- environmental conditions,
- and safety levels.

Avoid:
- excessive neon,
- overly dark cyberpunk themes,
- and high-saturation visual noise.

---

# Typography Direction

Typography should feel:
- modern,
- calm,
- readable,
- and spatially balanced.

Prioritize:
- large readable headings,
- concise recommendation cards,
- comfortable spacing,
- and clean visual rhythm.

The interface should feel breathable.

---

# Motion & Animation

Animations should be:
- subtle,
- smooth,
- and purposeful.

Examples:
- soft environmental transitions,
- map movement,
- weather condition fades,
- recommendation state transitions.

Avoid:
- excessive motion,
- flashy effects,
- or distracting interactions.

Motion should reinforce:
- calm intelligence,
- not visual spectacle.

---

# Mobile-First Philosophy

SIGAP should prioritize:

# Fast, contextual, glanceable experiences.

Users may access SIGAP while:
- commuting,
- outdoors,
- traveling,
- or making immediate decisions.

Therefore:
- information must be quickly understandable,
- interactions must be lightweight,
- and recommendations must be immediately visible.

---

# Emotional Experience

SIGAP should create the feeling that:

# “The city is understandable.”

The product should reduce:
- uncertainty,
- information overload,
- and environmental confusion.

Users should feel:
- supported,
- informed,
- and environmentally aware.

# An intelligent environmental layer that quietly helps humans navigate urban life more safely, comfortably, and intelligently.
